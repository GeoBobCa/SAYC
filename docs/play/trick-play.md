# How a hand is played

## Auction and contract { #auction-and-contract }

The dealer calls first, and the turn moves clockwise. A bid names a level and a denomination. Denominations rank from clubs through diamonds, hearts, spades and notrump. A later bid must outrank the last bid, either in denomination at the same level or by moving up a level. A player may also pass, double an eligible opponent's bid, or redouble an eligible opposing double. Four opening passes pass out the board. After a bid, three successive passes end the auction.

The last bid sets the contract, with a double or redouble applying if it remains in effect. The level adds to six to give the target number of tricks. For example, 4♥ needs ten tricks with hearts as trumps. In 3NT, nine tricks are needed and there is no trump suit.

## Declarer and dummy { #declarer-and-dummy }

Declarer is the first player on the declaring side to bid the final denomination. Declarer's left-hand opponent makes the opening lead. Declarer's partner then lays down dummy and declarer chooses the cards played from both hands.

## Following suit and winning a trick { #following-suit }

1. Play proceeds clockwise, with one card from each hand.
2. Follow the suit led whenever you hold a card in it. If you are void, you may discard or, in a suit contract, trump.
3. The highest trump played wins. If no trump is played, the highest card of the suit led wins.
4. The winning hand leads to the next trick. In duplicate play, each player keeps their played cards in order in front of them.

If something goes wrong at a duplicate table, call the director for a ruling.

## Making the contract { #making-the-contract }

At the end of 13 tricks, compare declarer's tricks with the target. Extra tricks are overtricks. Missing tricks are undertricks. In duplicate, level, denomination, vulnerability, doubling, and overtricks or undertricks affect the score.

| Contract category | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Partscore | 2♥ or 3♦ |
| Game | 3NT, 4♥, 4♠, 5♣ or 5♦ |
| Small slam | Any six-level contract, requiring 12 tricks |
| Grand slam | Any seven-level contract, requiring all 13 tricks |

The examples are undoubled contracts. A doubled or redoubled contract can earn game when its contract trick score reaches 100.
