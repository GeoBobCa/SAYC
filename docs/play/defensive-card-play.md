# Opening leads and signals

## Opening lead agreements { #opening-leads }

These agreements describe **which card** to lead once you have selected a suit. Use the auction to decide **which suit** to lead.

| Holding | SAYC lead agreement | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Touching honor sequence | Top of the sequence | K from K–Q–J–4 |
| Interior sequence | Top of the sequence within the suit | J from A–J–10–9 against notrump |
| Four or more cards with an honor and no applicable sequence | Fourth best | 4 from K–8–6–4 |
| Four or more without an honor | Second highest | 7 from 9–7–5–3 |
| Three low cards against a suit | Lowest | 2 from 8–6–2 |
| Three low cards against notrump | Highest | 8 from 8–6–2 |
| A–K–x against a suit | Ace | A from A–K–4 |

## Attitude signals { #attitude-signals }

With a choice of spot cards, a relatively high card encourages the suit and a low card discourages it. The same attitude agreement applies to discards. Read the signal in context. A defender with only one legal card in the suit has no choice.

## Count signals { #count-signals }

When giving count, play high then low with an even number of cards in the suit. Play low then high with an odd number. The auction, dummy and preceding plays help partner tell when count is relevant. Following suit always takes priority.
