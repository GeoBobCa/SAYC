# Responding with minor-suit support and a four-card major

When partner opens a minor, a four-card major may give you another fit. Start by asking which call tells partner what they need to know next.

## First principles

- **Fit.** A major-suit game needs ten tricks. A minor-suit game needs eleven. Showing your four-card major gives opener a chance to reveal four-card support.
- **Strength.** A new suit at the one level and a limit raise give different information. The new suit shows length and enough to respond. The direct limit raise shows both support and an invitational range.
- **Room.** A one-level response leaves space for opener to show support, rebid notrump, or describe shape. With a minor fit and game values, consider 3NT while there is still room to discuss the hands. Inspect the side suits and entries as well as the point count.

## Read the bid streams

These are uncontested auctions. Opponents pass throughout. The first two rows illustrate possible continuations, and the last row shows a limit-raise signoff.

| Introduce | Explore | Clarify | Decide |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1♦** Opens the minor | **1♥** Shows hearts | **2♥** Shows support at the two level | **Pass** With a minimum response, stop in the known fit |
| **1♦** Opens the minor | **1♥** Shows hearts | **1NT** Describes a balanced minimum here | **3NT** With game values and suitable holdings, choose notrump |
| **1♦** Opens the minor | **3♦** Shows a limit raise with diamond support | **Pass** Opener declines the invitation | Auction ends |
