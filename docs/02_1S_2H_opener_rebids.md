# Beginner Bridge Cheat Sheet — 1♠ Opening, 2♥ Response

**Important correction:** **1♠ – 1♥ is not a legal auction.** Hearts are lower-ranking than spades, so after partner opens **1♠**, a natural heart response is **2♥**.

**System:** SAYC with **12 total points** permitted to open one of a suit. Opponents pass.

## What do we know already?

- **Opener: 1♠** — **5+ spades**, at least **12 total points** under our agreement.
- **Responder: 2♥** — normally **5+ hearts and 10+ points**; **forcing for at least one round**, but **not automatically forcing to game** in SAYC. This is **not** the modern “2-over-1 game forcing” system.

## Opener's likely rebids

| Opener rebids | Typical meaning | Why choose it? | Responder's likely next action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2♠** | Usually **6+ spades**, a minimum hand (about **12–15** total points) | Shows extra spade length without overstating strength | **2NT** to invite with about **10–11 HCP** and a balanced hand; **3♥** with a long heart suit and limited values; **3♠** is game-forcing support in SAYC |
| **2NT** | **12–14 HCP**, balanced, generally without a heart fit | Shows balanced minimum rather than inventing a suit | **3♥** to emphasize **6+ hearts**; **3NT** with sufficient combined HCP |
| **3♥** | Usually **3+ hearts**, about **12–15** support points | Finds at least an eight-card heart fit (responder promised five) | Often **4♥** to play with a suitable fit; this can be an awkward borderline auction for minimum hands, so agree on your continuations |
| **3♣ / 3♦** | Usually **4+ cards** in the minor; may still be minimum or medium strength | Describes a real second suit | Prefer **3♠** if suitable, rebid hearts with extra length, or choose notrump with stoppers; do not assume opener is strong |
| **3♠** | Strong hand with **6+ spades**, generally **16–18** total points | Jump rebid shows extra playing strength | Game should be seriously considered, usually **4♠** or **3NT** depending on fit |
| **3NT** | Approximately **18–19 HCP** and balanced | Strong balanced hand; likely game in notrump | Usually **Pass**, unless a good major-suit fit is clearer |

### Important SAYC wrinkle

A new-suit response at the **two level** is more demanding than a one-level response. The ACBL SAYC booklet advises that responder **bid again after a two-level new-suit response unless opener has bid game**. A responder's later **3♠** bid is **game-forcing** in this setting; it is **not** the same as a weak preference to opener's spades.

### Example auctions

1. **1♠ – 2♥ – 2♠ – 2NT**: responder has a roughly invitational hand, no clear major-suit fit, and suggests notrump.
2. **1♠ – 2♥ – 2♠ – 3♠**: responder now shows **game-forcing** spade support — do not confuse this with a simple limit raise.
3. **1♠ – 2♥ – 2NT – 3NT**: opener shows a balanced minimum; responder has enough to choose game in notrump.

**Beginners' reminder:** Stayman and Jacoby transfers do **not** apply in this auction. Also, **SAYC two-over-one responses are not automatically game-forcing**.

**Source:** [ACBL SAYC System Booklet](https://web2.acbl.org/documentlibrary/play/SP3%20%28bk%29%20single%20pages.pdf), sections “Responses and Later Bidding” and “Subsequent Bidding by Responder.” Modified only to permit opening a suit with 12 total points.
