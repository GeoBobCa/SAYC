# Board 16 · Getting back to the South hand

[Start with the lesson](../../lessons/play/ruff-as-an-entry.md)

**Result** · 4♠ by South, down one. East–West vulnerable. Robot opponents.

## Make the decision

After two passes, East opened 1♦, South jumped to 3♠ and North raised to 4♠. At trick four, dummy won the ♦K. West had played the ♦Q and ♦10 on the first two diamond rounds. If you lead dummy’s last diamond, which trump will you use?

<details>
<summary>Reveal the position and the field result</summary>

At trick five, South ruffed with the ♠4 and West overruffed with the ♠8. West then cashed the ♣A, the defenders’ fourth trick. South ruffed East’s ♥J at trick nine, so the heart lead at that point did not cost the contract.

In the alternative, South ruffs the diamond with the ♠10, then leads the singleton club. West wins the ♣A and returns a heart in this illustrative continuation. South wins, leads a spade to dummy’s king to remove West’s remaining trump, and ruffs a club to get back to hand. The replay gives South ten tricks on that continuation.

Ten tables made 4♠ undoubled and one made it doubled.

</details>
