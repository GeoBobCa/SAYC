# Weak twos and preempts

## Weak-two openings { #weak-two-openings }

2♦, 2♥ and 2♠ show 5–11 HCP and usually a decent six-card suit. The booklet allows judgment with an unusually good five-card suit or a poor seven-card suit.

## Responding to a weak two { #weak-two-responses }

| Response | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Raise opener's suit | To play, possibly preemptive |
| 3NT | To play |
| New suit | At least five cards, forcing one round. With support, opener can raise a major response |
| 2NT | Forcing inquiry with game interest, including when still available after interference |

After 2NT, opener repeats the opening suit with a minimum (5–8). With a maximum, opener shows an outside ace or king by bidding its suit, or bids 3NT without an outside feature. For example, 2♥ – 2NT – 3♣ shows a maximum and the club ace or king.

## Three- and four-level openings { #opening-preempts }

Judge the expected tricks in your suit and the vulnerability. Opposite an unpassed partner, the booklet's rule of thumb allows a shortfall of roughly two tricks when vulnerable against nonvulnerable opponents, three at equal vulnerability and four when nonvulnerable against vulnerable opponents.
