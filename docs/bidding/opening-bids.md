## Choosing a suit { #choosing-opening-suit }

With two equally long five-card or six-card suits, open the higher-ranking one. With 4–4 in the minors, normally open 1♦. With 3–3 in the minors, normally open 1♣. With four cards in each major, three diamonds and two clubs, choose 1♦ when opening one of a suit. A balanced hand in the appropriate notrump range may contain a five-card major or minor.

A one-level suit opening shows opening strength. Seat, suit quality and distribution affect marginal decisions. The booklet describes normally five-card majors and allows occasional judgment, such as opening a four-card major in third seat.

## Valuing the hand { #hand-valuation }

Count A = 4, K = 3, Q = 2 and J = 1 for HCP. Evaluate balance for notrump. After a fit is known, suit length and shortness can affect how many tricks the partnership expects to take. Use [the point type specified for the bid](major-openings.md#major-raise-responses).
