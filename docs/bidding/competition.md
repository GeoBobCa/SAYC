# Competitive bidding

## Overcalls and responses { #overcalls }

| Action | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Simple suit overcall | About 8–16 points and normally a good five-card or longer suit |
| Direct 1NT overcall | Balanced 15–18, preferably with a stopper. 2♣ is Stayman and other responses are natural |
| Jump suit overcall | Preemptive, like an opening at that level |
| Jump to 2NT | At least 5–5 in the two lowest unbid suits |
| Cuebid of the opponents' only bid suit | Michaels. Over a minor it shows both majors. Over a major it shows the other major and an unspecified minor |
| Cuebid when opponents have shown two suits | Natural in either suit |

A cuebid by the overcaller's partner forces one round. A cheapest rebid of the overcalled suit shows a minimum. After a Michaels cuebid over a major, partner's 2NT asks for the minor. The booklet gives examples of 8+ points for both majors over a minor and 10+ for the other major and a minor over a major.

In balancing seat, suit actions can be lighter, and a reopening 1NT overcall shows about 10–15. Against an opposing 1NT, direct suit overcalls are natural on the Yellow Card. Against a weak two, the card lists 2NT as balanced 16–19.

## Takeout doubles { #takeout-doubles }

The booklet treats a double of an opening suit bid through 4♦ as takeout and a double of an opening suit bid of 4♥ or higher as penalty. A below-game jump response to a takeout double invites. A cuebid forces. Doubling and then bidding your own long suit can show a hand too strong for a simple overcall.

## Negative doubles { #negative-doubles }

After partner opens and an opponent overcalls a suit, negative doubles apply through 2♠. They typically show the unbid major or majors.

| Auction | What double shows |
| --- | --- |
| 1♣ – (1♦) | At least four cards in each major |
| 1♦ – (1♥) | Exactly four spades. A direct 1♠ shows five or more in this auction |
| 1♦ – (1♠) | Four hearts with 6+ points, or five hearts with 5–10 points |

A direct major bid at the two level or higher in these competitive sequences shows at least five cards and 11+ points.

## When opponents intervene over our opening { #after-an-overcall }

A cuebid of right-hand opponent's suit shows game-forcing values without an obvious natural bid. After 1♠ – (2♣), 3♣ is game forcing and usually shows a spade raise. Against an opposing conventional two-suited bid, a double can show 10+ points and a cuebid of a shown suit can force to game.

Conventions for uncontested auctions generally switch off after opposing action unless SAYC specifies an exception. For example, 1♥ – (2♠) – 2NT is natural, about 12–14 HCP. See the specific rules for [1NT interference](notrump.md#notrump-interference).

## After an opponent's takeout double { #after-a-takeout-double }

| Responder's action | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| New suit at the one level | Forcing for one round |
| New suit at the two level without a jump | Natural and nonforcing, about 6–10 points |
| 2NT | Limit raise or better |
| Jump raise | Preemptive with good support |
| Jump shift | Preemptive, usually a six-card or longer suit |
| Redouble | 10+ points, when a more descriptive action is unavailable |

Other redoubles depend on the auction. Over a double of your game-level contract or of a conventional call, redouble can be to play. With your side doubled for penalty at the three level or lower, a redouble can be SOS, asking partner to find a different suit.
