# Bridge Cheat Sheet: Understanding Jump Bids

**System:** Standard American (SAYC)  
**Level:** Beginner / Intermediate  
**Teaching sequence:** Introduce → Explore → Clarify → Decide

## The Big Idea

A **jump bid** is a bid at a higher level than necessary when a lower-level bid in the same denomination was available.

For example, after partner opens **1♥**:

- **2♥** — A simple raise, showing support.
- **3♥** — A jump raise, traditionally showing invitational strength.
- **4♥** — A double-jump raise, often showing a weaker, distributional hand rather than extra strength.

> **Remember:** A jump does **not** always mean a strong hand. Some jumps show strength, some invite game, and others are deliberately weak bids designed to make bidding difficult for the opponents.

## 1. Quick-Reference Table

The following are typical meanings in basic SAYC, primarily for uncontested auctions.

| Type of jump | Example auction | What it communicates |
|---|---|---|
| Opener's jump raise | 1♣ – 1♥ – 3♥ | Medium opener, about 16–18 points, heart support |
| Opener's jump rebid | 1♥ – 1♠ – 3♥ | Medium opener, about 16–18 points, usually 6+ hearts |
| Opener's jump shift | 1♦ – 1♥ – 2♠ | Very strong opener, approximately 19+ points, second suit |
| Responder's jump raise | 1♠ – 3♠ | Limit raise, approximately 10–11 support points |
| Responder's jump shift | 1♣ – 2♥ | Very strong responder, game forcing, slam interest |
| Responder's jump rebid | 1♣ – 1♥ – 1NT – 3♥ | Invitational hand with long hearts |
| Jump to notrump | 1♣ – 1♥ – 2NT | Strong balanced opener, typically 18–19 HCP |
| Jump overcall | (1♥) – 2♠ | Weak, long spade suit, preemptive |
| Preemptive jump raise | 1♠ – (Double) – 3♠ | Weak hand with good trump support |

*Point ranges are approximate. Opener's suit bids generally refer to total points; responder's raises to support points; and notrump bids to high-card points (HCP). Partnership agreements and competition can change meanings.*

## 2. Jumps Made by Opener — Clarify

Opener uses many jumps to show strength beyond the original opening bid.

### A. Jump Raise of Responder's Suit

**Auction: 1♣ → 1♥ → 3♥**  
**Meaning: Invitational**

| Stage | Bid | Message |
|---|---|---|
| Introduce | 1♣ | I have an opening hand. |
| Explore | 1♥ | I have at least four hearts and 6+ points. |
| Clarify | 3♥ | I have heart support and a medium-strength opener, usually 16–18 points. |

**What happens next?** Responder with a minimum hand can pass 3♥. With suitable game-going values, responder bids 4♥.

### B. Jump Rebid of Opener's Original Suit

**Auction: 1♥ → 1♠ → 3♥**  
**Meaning: Invitational**

| Stage | Bid | Message |
|---|---|---|
| Introduce | 1♥ | I have at least five hearts. |
| Explore | 1♠ | I have at least four spades. |
| Clarify | 3♥ | I have a strong heart suit, usually six or more cards, and approximately 16–18 points. |

**What happens next?** Responder decides whether to pass, bid 3NT, or bid 4♥, depending on strength and fit.

### C. Jump Shift into a New Suit

**Auction: 1♦ → 1♥ → 2♠**  
**Meaning: Game forcing**

| Stage | Bid | Message |
|---|---|---|
| Introduce | 1♦ | I have an opening hand with diamonds. |
| Explore | 1♥ | I have at least four hearts. |
| Clarify | 2♠ | I have a very strong opener, approximately 19+ points, and spades as well as diamonds. |

**What happens next?** Responder continues describing the hand. The partnership is generally committed to game.

> **Key distinction:** A jump **raise** supports partner's suit. A jump **rebid** emphasizes your original suit. A jump **shift** introduces a different suit.

## 3. Jumps Made by Responder — Explore and Decide

Responder's jumps matter because their meanings vary substantially.

### A. Jump Raise — The Limit Raise

**Auction: 1♠ → 3♠**  
**Meaning: Invitational**

| Stage | Bid | Message |
|---|---|---|
| Introduce | 1♠ | I have at least five spades and opening strength. |
| Explore | 3♠ | I have at least three-card spade support and approximately 10–11 support points. |

**What happens next?** Opener with a minimum hand passes 3♠. With enough extra strength, opener bids 4♠.

### B. Jump Shift — A Very Strong Response

**Auction: 1♣ → 2♥**  
**Meaning: Game forcing in traditional SAYC**

| Stage | Bid | Message |
|---|---|---|
| Introduce | 1♣ | I have an opening hand. |
| Explore | 2♥ | I have a very strong responding hand and a heart suit. We should investigate game and perhaps slam. |

In traditional SAYC, a direct jump shift by an unpassed responder is strong and suggests slam interest. **Some modern partnerships use weak jump shifts instead**, so partners must agree on the meaning.

### C. Jump Rebid — Extra Values and Suit Length

**Auction: 1♣ → 1♥ → 1NT → 3♥**  
**Meaning: Invitational**

| Stage | Bid | Message |
|---|---|---|
| Introduce | 1♣ | I have an opening hand. |
| Explore | 1♥ | I have at least four hearts. |
| Clarify | 1NT | I have a balanced, minimum opening hand, without adequate heart support to raise. |
| Decide | 3♥ | I have a long heart suit, normally six or more cards, and enough strength to invite game. |

**What happens next?** Opener may pass 3♥ or bid game with suitable values.

## 4. Double Jumps and Special Jumps

These can have quite different meanings from ordinary jumps.

| Example auction | Typical meaning |
|---|---|
| 1♥ – 4♥ | Often weak and distributional, with 5+ hearts in support; intended to play |
| 1♣ – 1♥ – 4♥ | Very strong opener, excellent heart support, enough to bid game |
| 1♥ – 2NT | **Jacoby 2NT** if agreed: 13+ support points, strong heart support, game forcing |
| 1NT – 2NT | Invitational, usually 8–9 HCP, balanced |
| 1NT – 4NT | Quantitative slam invitation, not Blackwood, in standard uncontested bidding |

Notice that **1♥ – 4♥** may be weak, while **1♣ – 1♥ – 4♥** shows a very strong opener. The bidding context changes everything.

*Jacoby 2NT is a convention and must be part of your partnership agreement.*

## 5. Jump Bids When Opponents Interfere

In competitive auctions, jumps are frequently used to interfere rather than show strength.

| Auction | Typical SAYC meaning |
|---|---|
| (1♦) – 2♠ | Weak jump overcall: long spades, preemptive |
| (1♥) – 3♣ | Weak jump overcall: long clubs, preemptive |
| 1♠ – (Double) – 3♠ | Weak, preemptive raise with spade support |
| 1♦ – (Double) – 2♥ | Weak jump shift, long hearts, intended to play |

*Parentheses indicate an opponent's bid or call.*

The same jump in an uncontested auction might promise considerable strength.

## 6. How to Interpret Any Jump Bid

Ask these four questions at the table:

1. **Who made the jump?** Opener, responder, or an opponent?
2. **What suit is being bid?** Partner's suit (raise), own suit (rebid), or a new suit (shift)?
3. **Has an opponent interfered?** Competition often changes the meaning of jumps.
4. **Is the jump forcing?** An invitation may be passed; a game force requires bidding to game.

## 7. Essential Memory Rules

| Jump type | Think |
|---|---|
| Jump raise by opener | “I have extra strength and support.” |
| Jump rebid by opener | “I have extra strength and a long suit.” |
| Jump shift by opener | “I have a very strong hand and another suit.” |
| Jump raise by responder | “Partner, I'm inviting you to game.” |
| Jump shift by responder | “I have a very strong hand; keep bidding.” |
| Weak jump overcall | “I have a long suit and want to obstruct the opponents.” |

**These are learning shortcuts, not universal rules.** Always consider the previous auction and partnership agreements.

## A Note on Our SAYC Variation

Our teaching variation allows **12 total points to justify an opening bid**. The basic jump structure remains unchanged. An opener at the bottom of the opening range should be cautious about making an invitational jump.

For this cheat sheet, preserve the conventional medium and maximum ranges rather than shifting every jump threshold downward.
