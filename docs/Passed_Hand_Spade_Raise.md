# How should a passed hand invite in spades?

*Improving club players · 15 minutes*

## Board 4 · Your call as North

West deals. Both sides are vulnerable.

West Pass → North Pass → East Pass → South **1♠** (Introduce) → West Pass → **North ?** (Explore)

**♠ AQ9** · **♥ 84** · **♦ 853** · **♣ AJ972**

Choose 2♠, 3♠, or another call under your agreements. Explain how many trumps you can promise and what your bid tells South about game interest.

??? note "Reveal the bidding discussion and recorded result"

    **This is an invitational hand.** North has 11 HCP, three spades headed by A–Q, and a side ace. With one-way Drury, bid **2♣** to show a good raise with at least three trumps. That leaves room for South to stop in 2♠. If you play without Drury and your direct 3♠ is invitational on three-card support, **3♠** describes the hand. If 3♠ requires four and you have no agreed three-card invitation, **2♠** is a practical choice, though it understates your strength.

    **Rule of thumb.** After you have passed, count your trumps and assess your useful honours before choosing a raise. With a good three-card fit, use your agreed invitation. If there is a way to invite below 2♠, partner can decline at the two level.

    **Agreement that changes the answer.** Check whether 2♣ is artificial Drury or natural. In one common two-way Drury agreement, 2♣ shows a good raise with exactly three trumps and 2♦ shows a good raise with four or more. Agree separately on the meaning of a direct 3♠. [Larry Cohen’s Drury notes](https://www.larryco.com/uploaded/pdf/pdfup_798.pdf)

    **Common exception.** A direct 3♠ can be a weak, preemptive raise by agreement. With four trumps and little outside strength, that bid can fit the hand. With invitational values, use the agreed stronger route.

    **Recorded auction.** North raised to 3♠ (Explore), and South bid 4♠ (Clarify). The pair’s agreement for 3♠ is unconfirmed. If it was invitational, **South should generally decline** with 11 HCP, five spades headed by the jack, and no singleton or void. South’s four hearts are worth considering, but the overall hand gives little reason to expect ten tricks opposite an invitation.

    **Traveller.** South played 4♠ down three. It was the only spade game among 16 tables. Five tables stopped in 3♠ and all went down. Two played 2♠ and one made it. Those results show how this deal played across the field. North’s invitation and South’s acceptance are two decisions to assess separately.

## Variation · One more trump

Same auction and vulnerability. Replace the ♥4 with the ♠4.

**♠ AQ94** · **♥ 8** · **♦ 853** · **♣ AJ972**

What does the fourth spade change? Choose a call if 3♠ is a natural invitation requiring four trumps, then choose a call under the two-way Drury agreement described above.

??? note "Reveal the variation"

    Bid **3♠** under the natural agreement or **2♦** under the stated two-way Drury agreement. You have the same 11 HCP, and the fourth trump and singleton heart improve the hand’s playing strength.

## Fresh hand · Make your call as opener

Both sides are vulnerable. After three passes, you open **1♠** as South (Introduce). West passes, North bids **2♣** (Explore), and East passes. Your agreement is one-way reverse Drury. North’s 2♣ shows an invitational raise with at least three spades, and your 2♠ rebid can decline the invitation.

**♠ KJ974** · **♥ AQ73** · **♦ 84** · **♣ J6**

What do you bid (Clarify)? Explain why your hand does or does not have enough extra to accept.

??? note "Reveal the fresh hand"

    Bid **2♠**. You have 11 HCP, five trumps and no singleton or void. Partner’s invitation gives you a chance to stop at the two level. The four-card heart suit offers some potential, but you would need more playing strength to accept.

## Independent check

You passed initially. Partner opens 1♠ in fourth seat, and the next player passes. You have invitational values and exactly three spades. Your pair plays one-way Drury. What do you bid, and why does the level matter?

??? note "Check your answer"

    Bid **2♣**. It shows a good raise with at least three spades and lets opener decline at **2♠**.
