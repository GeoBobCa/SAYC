# Counting winners and losers

When dummy comes down, pause before playing. Convert the contract to a trick target, then make a plan for reaching your winners before the defense can take enough tricks to set you.

## In notrump, start with winners

**3NT needs nine tricks.** Count immediate winners across both hands, suit by suit. Then ask where any missing tricks can come from and whether you can reach the hand that holds them.

For example, **♣KQJ10 opposite ♣432** supplies three club winners once the defenders’ ace is driven out. Count these as potential tricks while the ace is still out. Before leading clubs, check whether you can regain the lead and reach the established winners.

## In a suit contract, start with losers

**4♠ needs ten tricks, so you can lose three.** Usually start with the hand holding the longer trumps and count its potential losers, suit by suit, using the other hand’s high cards to cover them. Look for a way to dispose of any extra loser by ruffing, discarding it on a winner, or taking a finesse.

For example, **♦742 opposite ♦A5** leaves two potential diamond losers in the long-trump hand. If the short-trump hand can ruff the third round safely, you can remove one of them. Check whether the defenders can overruff and whether you need to delay drawing all the trumps.

## Recount as the hand changes

After a key trick, ask which enemy trumps or high cards remain, how many tricks you can still afford to lose, and which hand needs the lead.

**Quick check.** You have eight immediate winners in 3NT. Before driving out the ♣A to develop clubs, what two things do you need to check?
