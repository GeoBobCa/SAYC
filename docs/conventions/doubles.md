# Doubles

Before interpreting **Double**, identify who opened, who overcalled, and whose turn it is. Those details help you distinguish takeout, negative and penalty doubles.

| Type | Example | What partner should hear |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Takeout** | (1♦) – Double – (Pass) – ? | An opponent opened. The doubler ordinarily has opening strength, shortness in diamonds and support for the unbid suits. Choose a suit or another descriptive call. |
| **Negative** | 1♦ – (1♥) – Double | Partner opened and an opponent overcalled. In this SAYC auction, responder shows exactly four spades. A direct 1♠ would show at least five. |
| **Penalty** | (1♦) – Double – (Pass) – Pass – (Pass) | The takeout doubler’s partner has elected to defend 1♦ doubled. This is a conversion to penalty. |

Parentheses identify opponents’ calls. The examples read clockwise.

## What to do next

- **After partner’s takeout double**, a cheapest suit response can be weak. If your right-hand opponent passes, you normally bid. A pass to convert requires a hand suited to defending the doubled contract, including strong trumps.
- **After partner’s negative double**, use the information about the unbid major or majors to look for a fit. The SAYC Yellow Card uses negative doubles through an opposing **2♠** overcall, inclusive.
- **When defending for penalty**, estimate the tricks your side can take. Use the opponents’ auction and your likely defensive winners.

**Quick check.** Read **(1♦)–Double** and **1♦–(1♥)–Double** aloud. Who opened in each auction, and what suit or suits is the doubler suggesting?
