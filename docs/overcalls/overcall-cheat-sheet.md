# SAYC overcalls cheat sheet

[Demonstration hands](overcall-demonstrations.md) · [Open hands in BBO Handviewer](overcall-handviewer.md)

An **overcall** is an entry into the auction after an opponent opens. The partner of the overcaller is called the **advancer**. In `1D - 1H - 1S - 3H`, East overcalls `1H` and West advances with `3H`.

## Choosing an overcall

| Call | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple suit overcall | `1D - 1H` | Natural. The SAYC booklet gives 8–16 points. Usually a five-card or longer suit. At the low end, look for a suit you would welcome as an opening lead. Be more selective at the two level and when vulnerable. |
| Weak jump overcall | `1D - 2S` | Preemptive. A jump to the two level shows the kind of hand you would open with a weak two-bid, typically a reasonable six-card suit and 5–11 HCP. Here `1S` was available, so `2S` is a jump. |
| Direct 1NT overcall | `1D - 1NT` | Balanced, 15–18 points. Prefer a stopper in opener's suit. After a pass, `2C` is Stayman. Other suit responses are natural under booklet SAYC, so `2D` and `2H` are natural bids. |
| Michaels cue bid | `1D - 2D` or `1S - 2S` | Directly bidding opener's suit shows at least 5–5 in two suits. Over a minor, show both majors. Over a major, show the other major and an unspecified minor. After `1S - 2S - P`, `2NT` asks which minor. |
| Unusual 2NT | `1H - 2NT` | A direct jump to `2NT` shows at least 5–5 in the two lowest unbid suits. Over `1H`, those suits are clubs and diamonds. |

With a hand above the simple suit-overcall range and a long suit, SAYC's standard approach is to **double first, then bid your suit**. In balancing seat, after `1D - P - P`, a suit overcall can be lighter and `1NT` shows 10–15 points.

## Advancing a simple suit overcall

| Your hand | Example | What your call says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Modest values and support | `1D - 1H - 1S - 2H` | A natural, nonforcing raise. With three hearts, you can usually count on at least an eight-card fit. |
| Good support and weak values | `1D - 1H - 1S - 3H` | With the weak jump-raise agreement used on these pages, this is preemptive. Four-card support is a practical starting point. Check vulnerability and hand shape. |
| Enough strength to investigate game opposite a sound overcall | `1D - 1H - P - 2D` | The forcing cue bid of opener's suit asks partner about the overcall. After opener passes, an economical `2H` shows a minimum in this example. |
| A playable suit of your own | `1D - 1H - P - 1S` | A natural, nonforcing new suit. Partner may pass. |
| Little support and no useful bid | `1D - 1H - 1S - P` | Pass is available. Reassess if the auction comes back to you. |

A **jump** skips an available bid in the same denomination. After `1D - 1H - 1S`, both `2H` and `3H` are legal, so `3H` is a jump. After `1D - 1H - 2S`, `3H` is the cheapest available heart raise.

## Read a cue bid by seat

- `1D - 2D` is East's **Michaels overcall**, showing both majors.
- `1D - 1H - P - 2D` is West's **forcing advance**, asking East about the heart overcall.
